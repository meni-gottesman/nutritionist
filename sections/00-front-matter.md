# The Healthiest Diet: An Evidence-Based Guide

### *Everything we can reliably say about building the healthiest possible diet — and exactly what it depends on*

**Compiled June 2026.** This guide synthesizes the strongest available nutrition evidence — the WHO and FAO, the USDA/HHS *Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030* (released January 2026, which we treat as the primary US guideline; continuous with the *2020–2025* edition where unchanged), the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, the American Heart Association and American Diabetes Association, the EAT-Lancet Commission, Cochrane systematic reviews, and major randomized trials and cohort studies. Every substantive claim is cited, evidence strength is labeled honestly, and genuine controversies are flagged rather than papered over.

> ⚠️ **Important — please read.** This is general educational information, not medical or dietetic advice. It cannot account for your individual health, medications, allergies, or conditions. Nutrition science is probabilistic and evolving; much of it is observational and cannot prove cause and effect. **Before making significant dietary changes — especially if you are pregnant, managing a medical condition (diabetes, kidney, heart, or GI disease), taking medications, underweight, or have any history of disordered eating — consult a physician or a registered dietitian.** Nothing here is a substitute for individualized professional care.

## The one-page answer

If you read nothing else, read this. After decades of research and five independently built diet-quality scores that all point the same way, the science converges on a strikingly simple core. **There is no single "healthiest diet" — but there is a healthiest *pattern*, and it's the same for almost everyone:**

**Eat mostly plants, minimally processed, and don't overeat.** Concretely:

- **Build meals around vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, nuts, and seeds.** Aim for **≥400 g (about 5 servings) of fruit and vegetables a day** and **25–38 g of fiber**. This is the most consistent finding in all of nutrition.
- **Make your fats unsaturated** — olive oil, nuts, seeds, oily fish — instead of saturated, and **eliminate industrial trans fats** entirely.
- **Get enough protein** — the RDA floor is 0.8 g/kg/day, but the 2025–2030 US Dietary Guidelines now suggest ~**1.2–1.6 g/kg/day** for general adults (and older adults and athletes need more still; the size of that increase is debated — see §02). Favor fish, legumes, nuts, poultry, and dairy over red and especially processed meat.
- **Limit the proven harms:** added/free sugars (**<10% of calories**, ideally <5%), sodium (**<2,000 mg, ~5 g salt**), refined grains, processed meat, and **ultra-processed foods** in general.
- **Drink mostly water.** Coffee and tea are fine; treat sugar-sweetened drinks as a primary target and remember there is no clearly safe level of alcohol.
- **Match calories to your needs**, and prioritize foods that are filling per calorie (protein, fiber, water-rich whole foods).
- **The best pattern is the one you'll keep.** Mediterranean and DASH have the strongest evidence, but any minimally-processed, plant-forward diet that fits your culture, budget, and life works.

Following a high-quality pattern like this is associated with roughly **15–25% lower all-cause mortality** versus a typical low-quality diet — most of the achievable benefit, captured by a handful of habits.

**Then personalize the dials — not the foundation.** What changes from person to person is the *emphasis*, not the core: your life stage, sex, activity level, goals, metabolic health, genetics, medical conditions, culture, and budget. Section 08 and the master matrix in Section 20 lay out exactly which dials to turn — and §20's **adjustable-targets table** (plus the live calculator in the HTML version) turns those dials into your exact numbers.
